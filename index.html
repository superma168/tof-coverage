Geometry, formulas and assumptions

The lens is treated as a point source at height H aimed straight down (nadir), with a rectangular pyramid field of view of full angles α across the width and β along the length.

Floor coverage (ground plane, z = 0)

W_floor = 2 · H · tan(α / 2) L_floor = 2 · H · tan(β / 2)

Usable coverage for a person of height h

A standing person's highest point is the top of the head at z = h. The cone cross-section shrinks linearly with height, so the head stays inside the FOV only while its horizontal offset from the sensor axis is within the cross-section at z = h:

W_usable = 2 · (H − h) · tan(α / 2) L_usable = 2 · (H − h) · tan(β / 2) W_usable / W_floor = L_usable / L_floor = (H − h) / H

The person's feet sit directly below their head, so that same rectangle is the usable floor footprint. Area therefore scales with the square of the height ratio:

A_usable / A_floor = ((H − h) / H)²

Blind margin

margin_width = (W_floor − W_usable) / 2 = h · tan(α / 2) margin_length = (L_floor − L_usable) / 2 = h · tan(β / 2)

The blind margin depends only on the person's height and the FOV — not on the ceiling height. Raising the sensor grows the floor rectangle but the lost ring around it keeps the same width.

Assumptions and caveats

  • Sensor points straight down; tilt skews the footprint and is not modelled.
  • FOV is a rectangular pyramid, as quoted on most multi-zone TOF datasheets. A circular-cone lens would inscribe a smaller rectangle.
  • Detection is treated as geometric line of sight to the top of the head. Real detection also needs sufficient return signal, and the range needed at a corner is the slant distance, which is longer than H.
  • Zone resolution is ignored: with a coarse zone grid the outermost zone may straddle the boundary, so keep design margin.
  • A shorter person is covered over a larger area, a taller person over a smaller one. Size for the tallest occupant you must catch.
  • Lens distortion and any mounting recess are not modelled — enter the true lens height as H.
